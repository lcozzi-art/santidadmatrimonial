      The book

      Caminos de Santidad Matrimonial

      20 marital paths in 20 centuries · Paths of Marital Holiness

      The stories of forty couples who lived their marital vocation as a path to God, from the first Christians to our own day: kings and farmers, doctors and teachers, martyrs and heroic mothers. The podcast and this site derived from this research. The book is currently available in Spanish.

      The authors

      Paola and Leo Cozzi are the parents of four children and live in Los Cardales, near Buenos Aires. They are a couple consecrated to the Institute of Families of the Schoenstatt Apostolic Movement. Paola is an educational psychologist and has worked in several schools; Leo holds a degree in Business Administration and works as a process and quality consultant. Together they have worked in the formation of young people and couples, on the married vocation and Christian mission.

              About

              About this site

              This collection gathers married men and women, and married couples, who have been beatified, canonized or whose cause for beatification has been opened, based on the research by Leonardo and Paola Cozzi presented in their book “Caminos de Santidad Matrimonial” (Paths of Marital Holiness, in Spanish).

              Titles according to the process of beatification

              Saint
              Was canonized, and is venerated throughout the whole Church.
              Blessed
              Was beatified, usually after a confirmed miracle, or through recognition of martyrdom.
              Venerable
              The Church recognized that he or she lived the virtues to a heroic degree.
              Servant of God
              The cause for beatification has been formally opened.

              “Both spouses” means that both husband and wife have an open or concluded cause. Both are named in any case, even when only one of them has a cause, to underline the complementarity of husband and wife.

              The century is the one in which most of the marriage took place, and the country is given by today's borders. For the memorial date, if the cause is for both spouses we use their wedding date; otherwise, the date of death.

              In the case of Venerables and Servants of God, nothing here is meant to anticipate the judgment of Church authority on causes still in progress.

              The reference “Religious” is used broadly for priests, bishops, monks, abbots, nuns or abbesses. This may have happened during the marriage (in early cases, or among Eastern Catholics) or after being widowed.
